# Character Looks

Read this before every prompt except reverse prompting. The rules for when and how to design a look are in `SKILL.md` ("Designing the girl's look"). This file holds archetypes to draw from and the way to describe any outfit, including one the user specified.

## How to use

Pick one archetype that fits the scene, then adapt it. You can swap the outfit to suit the setting, change one color, or trade an accessory. The result should read as one coherent character. In the prompt, spread its features along the viewer's path as described in `SKILL.md` section 5, rather than pasting them as one block.

Rotate through the list across requests so different prompts don't all get the same character.

## Describing an outfit

Build an outfit description in three passes, from what the eye reads at a distance to what it notices up close.

**Shape.** Start with what the outfit is and the outline it gives her body, such as a long coat falling to her ankles, a short fitted dress, or a hoodie that swallows her frame. If there are layers, say which piece is on the outside, because the outer piece sets the silhouette.

**Surface.** Then give each visible piece its color, fabric, and any pattern or trim, like `a charcoal wool coat`, `a sheer white blouse with scalloped cuffs`, or `a cotton skirt printed with small lemons`. Fabric matters more than it seems, because it decides how the piece folds and catches light.

**State.** Finish with how the clothes are worn right now: a sleeve pushed to the elbow, a coat hanging open, a strap slipping, a hem lifted by wind. This is where clothing joins the pose and the story, so attach it to the body part or action involved.

Accessories come after the outfit, each tied to where it sits (see "Give small details a location" in `SKILL.md`).

Say everything about a piece in the one place it first appears, rather than naming it early and returning later to add its color or fabric. `a charcoal wool coat hanging open over her shoulders` reads cleaner than `a coat` early in the prompt followed by `the coat is charcoal wool` near the end.

When the user has specified an outfit, keep every detail they gave. The three passes only decide the order to write those details in, not what to change.

## Outfits by setting

Use these when the archetype's outfit doesn't fit the scene:

- **Home or apartment at night**: oversized T-shirt, hoodie, loungewear set, loose knit sweater
- **Rain or cold outdoors**: long coat, trench coat, scarf, hoodie under a jacket
- **Summer or beach**: sundress, swimsuit with a thin open shirt, shorts and a camisole
- **Work or city**: blouse and skirt, knit vest, fitted blazer
- **Traditional or festival**: yukata, kimono, qipao
- **Hotel, bedroom, or erotic scenes**: slip dress, bathrobe, lingerie, the partner's oversized shirt
